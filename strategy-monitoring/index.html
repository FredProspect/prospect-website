Trading strategy monitoring: how to spot drift before it costs you

Every strategy goes through losing periods. The hard question is whether a bad run is normal variation or a sign that something has changed. Strategy monitoring gives you a way to answer that calmly, with evidence rather than emotion.

Why live strategies drift

A strategy that tested well can stop behaving like its backtest for several reasons. Market conditions change, so a trend strategy struggles in a sideways market. Costs rise as spreads widen or liquidity thins. Other traders crowd the same idea. Or the original edge was partly overfitting all along. Monitoring is how you notice which of these is happening.

Step 1: write down what you expect

Before going live, record the strategy’s thesis and its expected behaviour from research: typical win rate, average trade, trade frequency, normal and worst drawdowns, and how long losing streaks usually last. Without this baseline, every bad week feels like a crisis and every good week feels like proof.

Step 2: what to watch

  • Drawdown versus history: is the current drawdown within the range seen in testing, or beyond it?
  • Trade frequency: a strategy suddenly trading far more or less often may be meeting different conditions.
  • Average trade and costs: rising costs or slippage can quietly erode results.
  • Win rate and payoff: a shift in either can signal that the pattern has weakened.
  • Exposure: is the strategy holding the positions you’d expect?

Step 3: normal variation or a broken strategy?

Compare live behaviour with the range you recorded, not with the backtest’s average. A drawdown deeper than anything seen in testing, combined with a change in how the strategy trades, deserves a closer look. A loss within the normal range usually doesn’t. Decide your review triggers in advance so you’re not deciding under pressure.

Step 4: what to do when a flag appears

A flag is a prompt to investigate, not an instruction to act. Check data and execution first; many apparent problems are operational. Then ask whether market conditions have shifted and whether the original thesis still makes sense. Options include reducing size, pausing, or continuing unchanged. What matters is that the decision is deliberate and recorded.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my trading strategy has stopped working?

Compare live results with the ranges you recorded from research. A drawdown beyond historical norms, combined with changes in trade frequency or behaviour, is a reason to investigate. A loss within the normal range usually is not.

What is strategy drift?

Strategy drift is when a live strategy’s behaviour moves away from what its research suggested, for example trading more often, paying higher costs or suffering deeper drawdowns.

Should I stop a strategy after a losing streak?

Not automatically. Decide your review rules in advance, check for operational problems, and compare the streak with what testing showed was normal before acting.
