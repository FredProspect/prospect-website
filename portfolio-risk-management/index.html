Portfolio risk management for traders running several strategies

Running more than one strategy can make your results steadier, or it can quietly stack the same risk several times over. Portfolio risk management is about seeing how your strategies interact, so diversification is real rather than assumed.

Why look beyond individual strategies?

Each strategy can look sensible on its own and still combine badly. Three strategies that all profit from rising stock markets are, in a sell-off, really one strategy with three times the size. Portfolio risk management looks at the whole picture: what you are exposed to, how strategies move together and where losses could cluster.

Correlation: useful, but only a starting point

Correlation measures how closely two return streams move together, from +1 (in lockstep) to −1 (opposite). Low correlation between strategies is generally helpful. But correlations change, and they often rise sharply in stressful markets, exactly when you need diversification most. Treat historical correlation as a clue, not a guarantee.

Hidden concentration

Concentration hides in shared dependencies: the same market, the same sector, the same direction, the same holding period or the same liquidity conditions. Ask of each strategy: what has to happen in the market for this to make money, and for it to lose? If several answers are the same, you are more concentrated than the number of strategies suggests.

Position sizing across strategies

Sizing is where portfolio risk becomes real. Common approaches include risking a fixed fraction of the account per trade, scaling positions by volatility so each strategy contributes similar risk, and setting an overall limit on total exposure. Whatever you choose, decide it in advance and check how much you could lose if several strategies hit their worst days together.

A simple portfolio risk checklist

  • List what each strategy depends on to make money.
  • Group strategies by shared exposure: market, direction and timeframe.
  • Check correlations over calm and stressful periods.
  • Estimate the combined worst-case drawdown, not just each one separately.
  • Set a total risk limit and review it when you add a strategy.

Frequently asked questions

What is portfolio risk management in trading?

It is managing the combined risk of all your positions and strategies, including how they move together, shared exposures and total potential loss, rather than judging each one in isolation.

Does running more strategies reduce risk?

Only if the strategies are genuinely different. Strategies that depend on the same market moves can add concentration rather than diversification.

Why do correlations rise in a market crash?

In stressful markets many assets are sold together as traders reduce risk, so relationships that looked independent in calm periods can converge.
